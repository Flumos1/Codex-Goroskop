# Compatibility Safety Boundaries

Compatibility output must be useful without becoming deterministic.

## Avoid

- declaring two people compatible or incompatible as a final verdict;
- saying a relationship must begin, end, marry, separate, or fail;
- blaming a parent, child, partner, or friend through chart language;
- treating a child as fixed, doomed, difficult, or inferior;
- using fear, shame, or fatalistic labels;
- replacing medical, legal, financial, or family-professional advice.

## Prefer

- describe relationship patterns as tendencies and interaction themes;
- separate ease from friction instead of collapsing the pair into a verdict;
- name the relationship type and source system;
- use respectful language for children and family roles;
- offer reflection, boundaries, care, and practical communication prompts.

## Public Wording Pattern

Use formulations like:

- "This pairing may emphasize..."
- "A constructive way to use this contrast is..."
- "The likely friction point to watch is..."
- "This does not decide the relationship; it highlights where attention may help."
