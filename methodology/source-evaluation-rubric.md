# Source Evaluation Rubric

Use this rubric before turning any book, article, video, or website into project methodology.

## Evaluation Dimensions

### 1. Tradition

Identify the source tradition:

- Classical Western.
- Medieval Arabic/Persian.
- Hellenistic.
- Modern psychological.
- Jungian/archetypal.
- Vedic/Jyotish.
- Esoteric/spiritual.
- Medical or other special topic.
- Popular/commercial.

### 2. Use Case

Mark what the source is good for:

- Foundational doctrine.
- Natal chart interpretation.
- Houses and life areas.
- Planetary symbolism.
- Aspects.
- Transits and cycles.
- Predictive methods.
- Psychological framing.
- Spiritual practices.
- Style/reference language.
- Caution-only comparison.

### 3. Reliability Level

- `A`: foundational or historically important source.
- `B`: serious interpretive source with coherent method.
- `C`: useful practical or popular source, but needs cross-checking.
- `D`: speculative, commercial, medically risky, or methodologically weak.

Reliability does not mean scientific proof. It means usefulness and seriousness inside the symbolic astrology project.

### 4. Extraction Rules

When extracting ideas:

- Separate observation, doctrine, interpretation, and recommendation.
- Preserve the tradition context.
- Do not merge Western and Vedic rules unless explicitly marked as a synthesis.
- Do not convert medical astrology into health advice.
- Prefer psychological and reflective wording over fatalistic claims.
- Flag contradictions between schools instead of hiding them.

### 5. Product Safety

The final site should avoid:

- Direct medical diagnosis.
- Guaranteed predictions.
- Fear-based fatalism.
- Manipulative relationship claims.
- Financial, legal, or health decisions framed as destiny.

The final site may offer:

- Symbolic interpretation.
- Reflective questions.
- Probable themes of a period.
- Psychological patterns.
- Practical self-observation prompts.
- Spiritual or contemplative framing.
