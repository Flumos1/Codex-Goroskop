## Project Interpretation

### 1. Compatibility Is Contextual

The same pair should not receive one universal verdict.

Project rule:

- Always ask or infer relationship type.
- A romantic pairing, parent-child bond, and work partnership require different wording.

### 2. Compatibility Is Multi-Axis

The source supports thinking in several relationship categories rather than one score.

Generator use:

- Attraction / ease.
- Friction.
- Communication.
- Emotional climate.
- Family or care dynamics.
- Practical cooperation.
- Growth task.

### 3. Relationship Language Must Avoid Determinism

The book uses strong compatibility framing, but the project should soften public output.

Project rule:

- No "perfect match" or "doomed pair" claims.
- No deterministic advice to stay, leave, marry, divorce, trust, or reject.
- Use reflective and practical language.

### 4. Family Compatibility Needs Extra Care

Parent-child and family readings can easily become blame.

Project rule:

- Avoid assigning fault to parent or child.
- Use needs, roles, expectations, and communication patterns.
- Keep safety boundaries for abuse, coercion, and harm: astrology must not override real-world safety.

## Generator Rules To Add Later

### Rule: Relationship Type Declaration

Every compatibility report starts by naming the relationship type:

- Romantic.
- Marriage / long-term partnership.
- Parent-child.
- Mother-child.
- Father-child.
- Friends.
- Work or business.
- Family / relatives.

### Rule: Multi-Axis Compatibility

Compatibility output should use sections:

1. Natural ease.
2. Main friction.
3. Emotional needs.
4. Communication.
5. Practical cooperation.
6. Growth task.
7. Boundary / safety note.

### Rule: No Verdict Compatibility

The report must not collapse the relationship into a final score or verdict.

Use:

- "This pairing may..."
- "A recurring theme is..."
- "The relationship benefits from..."

Avoid:

- "This will work."
- "This cannot work."
- "You are destined."
- "You should leave / stay."
